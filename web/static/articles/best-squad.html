Does the best squad win the IPL?

By Fine Leg XI · 23 November 2026

Every IPL season produces the same argument at the end of it. Did the best team win, or did the playoffs pick a winner from a handful of good sides more or less at random? A short knockout gives a lot of room for one good night, and the format lets the fourth-placed side win the title with a run of three matches.

Fine Leg XI rates every player in every squad, so the question can be put to numbers. We took all 19 seasons in the ball-by-ball record, 2008 to 2026, ranked each season's squads by strength, and looked at where the champion stood.

How a squad is rated

Each player-season in the game carries a rating from 70 to 99 (how it is worked out is in how the ratings work). A squad's strength here is the average rating of its best twelve players: an eleven and one more, the size of a side once the Impact Player arrived. Using the best twelve rather than the whole squad stops a side being marked down for carrying young players who hardly played.

One honest warning before the results. A player's rating comes from the same season it describes. A side that won the title had players who had good seasons, partly because they won. So this is not a prediction made before a ball was bowled. It is a measure of how good each squad turned out to be. Keep that in mind, because it makes one of the findings below more striking, not less.

The results

The strongest squad of the season won the title in 8 of the 19 seasons. The second strongest won it 7 times, so 15 of the 19 titles went to one of the two strongest sides. No champion ranked lower than 4th in its own season.

What it means

Two readings pull in different directions, and both are true.

The first is that quality wins. A tournament with eight to ten teams, a fourteen-match league and a knockout at the end could in principle crown almost anyone. Instead the title went to one of the two strongest squads in most seasons, and never to a side outside the top four. The league stage does real work: it sends the strong sides through, and since 2011 the top two finishers have had a second chance if they lose Qualifier 1.

The second is that being the best is not enough. The strongest side failed to win more often than it won. And remember the warning above: these ratings already give champions some credit for winning. Even with that head start, the strongest squad still lost the title in most seasons.

The best side never to win

The strongest squad in the whole record, across all nineteen seasons, is Kings XI Punjab 2014, with an average of 86.2 across its best twelve. It did not win the title. That was the season of Glenn Maxwell's 552 runs off 294 balls, and the side topped the league stage before losing the final to Kolkata Knight Riders.

Royal Challengers Bangalore appear in the strongest-squad column more than once, too. On paper and in the record of individual seasons, their sides of the mid-2010s were as strong as anyone's. The title did not follow until much later.
