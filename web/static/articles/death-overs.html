Who really owns the death overs?

By Fine Leg XI · 23 November 2026

Overs 16 to 20 are where IPL matches are won and lost, and where reputations are made. Ask fans who the great death bowlers are and the same names come back: Malinga, Bumrah, Bravo, Bhuvneshwar. But "great at the death" can mean two different things. One bowler takes wickets and stops a big finish before it starts. Another concedes so little that the batters cannot catch up.

We separated the two. This article compares every bowler who has bowled at least 80 overs at the death in a full-length IPL innings, 37 bowlers in all, on how often they take a wicket and how much they concede.

What the death costs everyone

First, the baseline. Across every death over in the archive, a wicket falls every 13.5 balls and runs go at 9.88 an over. Those are the numbers an average bowler gives his captain in the last five overs. Runs here count everything charged to the bowler (off the bat, wides and no-balls, but not byes or leg-byes), and a wicket only counts if it is his, so a run out is not.

Of every bowler with 80 or more death overs, Sunil Narine is the cheapest: in 181 death overs he has gone at 7.31 an over. Lasith Malinga is next at 7.86, and Jasprit Bumrah at 8.19. Bowling at the death and conceding barely more than eight an over is what lets a captain set a defensive field and know the target is safe.

The wicket-takers are mostly a different group. Harshal Patel takes a death wicket every 10.6 balls, more often than 86% of the field, but concedes 10.06 an over, worse than the death average. Strike bowlers at the death often work that way: they attack the stumps, take wickets, and pay for the balls that miss.

Doing both

Very few bowlers are near the top of both lists. Counting the bowlers in the best quarter of this field for wickets AND for economy, the list is short: Ashish Nehra, Chris Morris, Lasith Malinga. Of those, Malinga did it over the longest stretch, 186 death overs: a wicket every 10.3 balls, better than 89% of the field, at 7.86 an over, better than 95%. On this evidence he is the best death bowler the IPL has had.

Bumrah is a closer call than his reputation suggests. His economy at the death, 8.19, beats 92% of the field, but he takes a death wicket every 14.1 balls, which is slightly less often than average. His value at the death is control, not wickets. Narine is the same: the cheapest of all, and close to average for wickets (13.0 balls a wicket).

Bravo and Bhuvneshwar: volume

Dwayne Bravo is the name most people reach for first, and the totals explain why: he has 115 death wickets, second only to Bhuvneshwar Kumar's 118. Measured per ball, Bravo was good rather than great: a wicket every 12.0 balls (better than 70% of the field) at 9.36 an over (better than 62%). His record at the death is built on how many overs his captains gave him, 230 of them, more than anyone but Bumrah and Bhuvneshwar.

Bhuvneshwar Kumar is similar. His 118 death wickets are the most anyone has taken, from 265 death overs, the most anyone has bowled. Per ball he sits close to the middle of this field: a wicket every 13.5 balls, 9.30 an over. Being trusted with that many death overs for that many seasons is a skill of its own, but it is durability, not dominance.

How this was measured

The death is overs 16 to 20 of a full-length innings; rain-shortened innings and super overs are left out. Every figure comes from the ball-by-ball record of every IPL match from 2008 to 2026. Percentages compare each bowler with the other 37 in the field, so "better than 70%" means better than 70% of the bowlers who bowled at least 80 death overs. In Fine Leg XI, a bowler's season card carries how his wickets fall across the innings, so a real death strike bowler is played that way by the match engine. You can find the best of them in the draft and the auction.
