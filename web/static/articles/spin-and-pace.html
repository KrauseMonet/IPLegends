Spin, pace and the shape of a T20 innings

By Fine Leg XI · 23 November 2026

Watch enough IPL cricket and the pattern feels obvious. Fast bowlers take the new ball, the spinners come on once the fielding restrictions end, and the quicks return for the last overs. But how strong is that pattern, really? Do captains bowl spin at the death? Has the balance moved as the game has got faster?

We went through every legal ball bowled in a full-length IPL innings from 2008 to 2026 and sorted them by who bowled them: a spinner or a pace bowler. Every bowler in the archive has a recorded style, so nothing here is guessed.

The shape is clear. Spin bowls 14% of the first over, then grows slowly through the powerplay. The moment the fielding restrictions lift, it jumps: from the seventh over spinners bowl most of the overs, peaking at 63% of over 9. From there spin fades steadily, and by the last over it is down to 8%.

Across the three phases, spin bowls 18% of the powerplay, 55% of the middle overs and 21% of the death.

What each costs

Who bowls an over is a captain's choice. What it costs is the result. Economy is runs charged to the bowler per over (off the bat, wides and no-balls, not byes or leg-byes); the last two columns are how many balls each style needs per wicket.

In the middle overs spin is clearly cheaper: 7.57 an over against pace's 8.11, while taking wickets about as often (24.8 balls a wicket against 24.6). That is the case for spin in the middle of a T20 innings in one line. With the field spread, a spinner gives batters very little pace to work with.

The death is more surprising. Spinners there go at 9.04 an over, while pace goes at 10.10. On the face of it, captains should bowl more spin at the death. But be careful with that number. Only the spinners captains trust most are given death overs at all, so spin at the death is a hand-picked group of the best, while pace at the death is everyone. The cheaper figure says more about who gets selected than about spin in general.

How the balance has moved

In 2008, the first season, spinners bowled 23% of all balls. By 2019 that had risen to 42%, the highest in any season. By 2026 it had fallen back to 31%. The middle years of the IPL were its spin era, and the recent years of record totals have come with captains turning back towards pace, though spin still bowls more than it did in the first season.

In the game

The Fine Leg XI match engine bowls its overs in this same shape. A side spreads its spin overs across the innings in the proportion real captains do, so your openers mostly face pace, your middle order faces the spinners, and your finishers face pace again. That is why a batter who plays spin well is worth more in the middle order, and why a side short of a good spinner leaks runs through overs 7 to 15. Build a side in the draft and see how it copes.
