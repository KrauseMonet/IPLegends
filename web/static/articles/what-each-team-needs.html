IPL 2027 auction: what every team needs

By Fine Leg XI · 27 November 2026

The ten franchises go into the IPL 2027 auction with ₹297.95 cr and 66 places to fill. We went through every ball of IPL 2026 to see where each side lost matches, what walked out of the door with its releases and trades, and which players on the list fill the hole.

Each team is ranked against the other nine in the three phases of an innings: the powerplay (overs 1 to 6), the middle overs (7 to 15) and the death (16 to 20). The league scored at 10.08, 9.27 and 10.75 runs an over in them in 2026. Candidates are judged on their IPL record from 2024 to 2026. The teams run in the order of the money they have left.

Lucknow Super Giants

₹47.45 cr for 7 places · won 4 of 14 in 2026

No side was worse in the middle overs. Lucknow scored 7.84 an over there, 10th of ten, and lost a wicket every 17.1 balls, also 10th. Against spin in those overs they scored 118 runs per 100 balls, the slowest in the league. With the ball, the death was the problem: a wicket every 18.1 balls, 10th. The powerplay attack was the best in the league at 9.00 an over, and most of it has gone: Mohammed Shami, who bowled 27 of those overs, is now at Rajasthan, so Lucknow keep only 58% of their powerplay overs. Rishabh Pant, the 2026 captain, has been traded to Delhi, and a new captain is still to be confirmed.

  • A death bowler. Avesh Khan took 19 death wickets at 9.91 an over from 2024 to 2026; Matheesha Pathirana 15 at 10.08.
  • A new-ball seamer to replace Shami: Trent Boult went at 8.26 in the powerplay, Deepak Chahar at 8.52.
  • A middle-order batter who scores off spin. Daryl Mitchell scored at 159 against spin in overs 7 to 20, Nitish Rana at 155.

Kolkata Knight Riders

₹41.65 cr for 8 places · won 6 of 14 in 2026

Kolkata had the cheapest middle-overs bowling in the league (8.14 an over) and took death wickets quicker than anyone, one every 9.8 balls. The batting held them back. They were 9th for powerplay scoring at 8.91 an over, and Ajinkya Rahane, the captain, has retired, taking 327 runs off 242 balls with him: only 71% of Kolkata's powerplay runs were scored by players still in the squad. The middle order is slow against spin, too. Rinku Singh, Rovman Powell, Ramandeep Singh and Manish Pandey together scored at 132 against spin in overs 7 to 20 from 2024 to 2026, against a league rate of 144.

  • A captain. Nitish Rana led Kolkata in 2023 and is on the list.
  • An attacking opener. Pathum Nissanka scored at 169 in the powerplay, Jonny Bairstow at 161.
  • A middle-order batter who plays spin. Daryl Mitchell (159 against spin) or Rana (155), who would cover the captaincy as well.

Sunrisers Hyderabad

₹40.15 cr for 7 places · out in the playoffs in 2026

Hyderabad scored fastest of anyone in the middle overs (10.12 an over) and bowled the cheapest death overs (10.02). Two things let them down: the most expensive powerplay bowling in the league (11.10) and a wicket every 10.2 balls at the death, 10th. Travis Head, who scored 410 off 241 balls, has opted out of IPL 2027, and only 63% of their powerplay runs came from players they kept. Khaleel Ahmed arrives from Chennai for the new ball and T Natarajan from Delhi for the death. The squad holds 2 specialist spinners.

  • An opener to replace Head. Jake Fraser-McGurk scored at 207 in the powerplay, Nissanka at 169.
  • A spinner. Maheesh Theekshana went at 7.87 an over in the middle overs.
  • Lower-order batting that survives the death.

Delhi Capitals

₹39.5 cr for 9 places · won 7 of 14 in 2026

Delhi have the most to rebuild. Their powerplay batting was the slowest in the league (8.67 an over) and their middle-overs bowling the most expensive (9.98). Then the players went. Only 42% of their powerplay runs were scored by players still in the squad, with Pathum Nissanka (278 runs) released. With the ball they keep 52% of their middle overs and 55% of their death overs: Mitchell Starc has opted out, Kuldeep Yadav has gone to Lucknow and T Natarajan to Hyderabad. Against spin in the middle overs they scored at 128, against 159 off pace. Rishabh Pant arrives from Lucknow beside Axar Patel, who led them in 2026.

  • Two openers. Nissanka (169 in the powerplay) and Abishek Porel (148, and a keeper) are both on the list.
  • A middle-overs spinner for Kuldeep's 37 overs: Theekshana went at 7.87.
  • A death seamer. Avesh (9.91) or Boult (9.73).

Mumbai Indians

₹32.6 cr for 9 places, only 2 for overseas players · won 4 of 14 in 2026

Mumbai's new-ball attack has gone. Deepak Chahar, Trent Boult and Hardik Pandya are gone, and Mumbai keep only 46% of their powerplay overs and 38% of the wickets taken in them. Arshdeep Singh arrives from Punjab. Allah Ghazanfar, who took 13 wickets, has also been released. At the death they were 8th for scoring at 10.26 an over, and Hardik's departure leaves 2 finishers. Rohit Sharma, who led them to five titles, has been named captain again.

  • Indian seamers, because there are so few overseas places: Chahar went at 8.52 in the powerplay, Avesh took 19 death wickets.
  • A spinner for the middle overs.
  • An Indian finisher. Shahrukh Khan struck at 179 at the death, Nehal Wadhera at 160.

Chennai Super Kings

₹28.05 cr for 6 places · won 6 of 14 in 2026

Chennai lost powerplay wickets faster than any side, one every 16.8 balls, and were 9th for middle-overs scoring at 8.58 an over. Their middle-overs bowling was second best in the league. The new ball is the hole: Khaleel Ahmed (13 powerplay overs) has gone to Hyderabad and two overseas seamers were released, leaving 64% of the powerplay overs with players still in the squad. MS Dhoni has retired; Sanju Samson is in the squad and Ruturaj Gaikwad led them in 2026. The squad holds only 2 finishers.

  • A new-ball seamer. Boult (8.26) or Chahar (8.52).
  • A finisher. Wadhera (160 at the death) or Mahipal Lomror (200).
  • Middle-overs runs against spin, where they scored at 132.

Rajasthan Royals

₹27.05 cr for 6 places · out in the playoffs in 2026

Rajasthan scored fastest of anyone in the powerplay, 11.38 an over. After that the wickets came quickly: a wicket every 19.9 balls in the middle overs (9th) and every 10.9 at the death (8th). They lost almost nobody and added Mohammed Shami and Manimaran Siddharth to the attack, so the auction is about depth. Riyan Parag, who led them in 2026, is in the squad.

  • A middle-order batter: they hold 2 against the 4 a full squad wants.
  • A death hitter. Lomror (200) or Wadhera (160).
  • A third keeper.

Punjab Kings

₹18.6 cr for 6 places · won 7 of 14 in 2026

Punjab scored fastest at the death (12.55 an over) and bowled the most expensive death overs (11.97). Arshdeep Singh, who bowled 26 powerplay and 13 death overs, has gone to Mumbai, so Punjab keep 60% of their powerplay overs and 47% of the wickets in them. Hardik Pandya arrives from Mumbai beside Shreyas Iyer, who led them in 2026. On paper the squad still holds 7 pace bowlers, but a count cannot see what Arshdeep was worth.

  • A new-ball seamer. Boult took 21 powerplay wickets at 8.26.
  • A death bowler. Avesh (19 death wickets) or Pathirana (15).
  • A third keeper.

Royal Challengers Bengaluru

₹14.15 cr for 5 places, 1 for an overseas player · champions in 2026

The champions kept nearly everyone who played for them, and ranked in the top four in every phase with the bat. The one soft spot was taking middle-overs wickets: one every 23.8 balls, 7th. The real question is age. At an average of 32.1 theirs is the oldest retained squad in the league, and it holds only 1 middle-order batter and 2 specialist spinners.

  • A young Indian middle-order batter to build the next side around.
  • A wicket-taking spinner.

Gujarat Titans

₹8.75 cr for 3 places · runners-up in 2026

Gujarat kept nearly their whole side and added Harshal Patel from Hyderabad. Their bowling took middle-overs wickets more often than anyone, one every 16.2 balls. Their batting at the death was the slowest of all (10.00 an over), and the squad holds just 2 finishers. With so little money there is room for one buy that matters.

  • An Indian death hitter. Lomror struck at 200 at the death, Wadhera at 160.

Every number is counted from the IPL ball-by-ball archive (full-length innings, super overs left out): a wide is not a ball faced, a bowler is charged wides and no-balls but not byes or leg-byes, and only the wickets that are a bowler's own count to them. "Specialist" bowlers are bowlers and all-rounders, not batters who bowl the odd over. Squads, money and places are as the retentions left them on 17 November. Captaincy news and the players who have opted out are as reported, not yet confirmed by an official register, which arrives on 3 December.
