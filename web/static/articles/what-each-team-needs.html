IPL 2027 auction: what every team needs

By Fine Leg XI · 27 November 2026 · updated 28 November

The ten franchises go into the IPL 2027 auction with ₹297.95 cr and 66 places to fill. We went through every ball of IPL 2026 to see where each side lost matches, what walked out of the door with its releases and trades, and which players on the list fill the hole. Then we read what the franchises, their coaches and the pundits have said since the retentions, because a scorecard cannot see a new coach, an injury or a player being groomed for a job.

Each team is ranked against the other nine in the three phases of an innings: the powerplay (overs 1 to 6), the middle overs (7 to 15) and the death (16 to 20). The league scored at 10.08, 9.27 and 10.75 runs an over in them in 2026. Candidates are judged on their IPL record from 2024 to 2026. The teams run in the order of the money they have left.

Lucknow Super Giants

₹47.45 cr for 7 places · won 4 of 14 in 2026

No side was worse in the middle overs, with bat or ball. Lucknow scored 7.84 an over there, 10th of ten, and lost a wicket every 17.1 balls, also 10th. Against spin in those overs they scored 118 runs per 100 balls, the slowest in the league. Their bowlers took a middle-overs wicket only once every 27.0 balls, and a death wicket every 18.1: 10th both times. The powerplay attack was the best in the league at 9.00 an over, and its leader has gone: Mohammed Shami, who bowled 27 of those overs, is now at Rajasthan, so Lucknow keep only 58% of their powerplay overs.

Beyond the numbers

Rishabh Pant gave up the captaincy after the season and was then traded to Delhi for Kuldeep Yadav, who took 10 wickets for Delhi in 2026. Tom Moody, Lucknow's global director of cricket, has said the middle overs are exactly where they struggled to take wickets, and that Kuldeep is part of the answer. Moody also named Mitch Marsh, Aiden Markram and Nicholas Pooran as the contenders to captain, and Lucknow may reach the auction without naming one. The Shami trade brought back Sam Curran, who missed 2026 injured; Moody said Lucknow wanted an all-rounder who can bat in the middle order and bowl with both the new ball and at the death, and that Prince Yadav, Mohsin Khan and Mayank Yadav are expected to step up. Wisden names Lucknow as one of three teams that could spend big on Ben Stokes.

  • A middle-order batter who scores off spin. Daryl Mitchell scored at 159 against spin in overs 7 to 20 from 2024 to 2026, Nitish Rana at 155.
  • A death bowler. Avesh Khan, whom Lucknow released, took 19 death wickets at 9.91 an over from 2024 to 2026; Matheesha Pathirana 15 at 10.08.
  • A new-ball seamer, only if Curran and the young Indian quicks are not enough. Trent Boult went at 8.26 in the powerplay.

Kolkata Knight Riders

₹41.65 cr for 8 places · won 6 of 14 in 2026

Kolkata had the cheapest middle-overs bowling in the league (8.14 an over) and took death wickets quicker than anyone, one every 9.8 balls, and the men who bowled those death overs are all still there: Kolkata keep 97% of them. The batting held them back. They were 9th for powerplay scoring at 8.91 an over. Ajinkya Rahane, the captain, opened in 13 of their 14 innings, scoring 327 off 242 balls, and has retired; Finn Allen, who opened in 11, is retained. The middle order is slow against spin. Rinku Singh, Rovman Powell, Ramandeep Singh and Manish Pandey together scored at 132 against spin in overs 7 to 20 from 2024 to 2026, against a league rate of 144.

Beyond the numbers

ESPNcricinfo reported that Rinku Singh, vice-captain in 2026, is the leading candidate to take over, with Sunil Narine and Rovman Powell also mentioned, and head coach Abhishek Nayar has praised Rinku's growth as a leader. Aakash Chopra and Abhinav Mukund have both said they would make Rinku captain. Kolkata's 2026 was also hurt by injuries: Harshit Rana, retained, played no match after knee surgery, and Matheesha Pathirana, released, bowled 1 over all season before a hamstring injury. One preview has Kolkata chasing an overseas death bowler; on 2026's numbers that is the one part of the side that worked. Wisden suggests Ben Stokes could captain, add balance and open beside Allen.

  • An opener to go with Allen. Pathum Nissanka scored at 169 in the powerplay, Jonny Bairstow at 161.
  • A middle-order batter who plays spin. Daryl Mitchell (159 against spin) or Nitish Rana (155).
  • A captain, if it is not Rinku. Rana led Kolkata in 2023 and is on the list.

Sunrisers Hyderabad

₹40.15 cr for 7 places · out in the playoffs in 2026

Hyderabad scored fastest of anyone in the middle overs (10.12 an over) and bowled the cheapest death overs (10.02). Two things let them down: the most expensive powerplay bowling in the league (11.10) and a wicket every 10.2 balls at the death, 10th. Travis Head opened in all 15 of their innings and scored 410 off 241 balls; only 63% of their powerplay runs came from players they kept. Khaleel Ahmed and Nathan Ellis arrive from Chennai, and T Natarajan from Delhi. Going the other way: Liam Livingstone to Chennai, Harshal Patel to Gujarat (13 overs without a wicket in 2026) and the leg-spinner Zeeshan Ansari to Delhi. The squad holds 2 specialist spinners.

Beyond the numbers

Head has opted out of IPL 2027, and the statement Hyderabad posted gives international ambitions and family time as the reasons. Pat Cummins, captain in 2026, is retained at ₹18 cr, but missed the first seven matches last season after a back injury, with Ishan Kishan captain in the meantime; Cricbuzz reported a teammate saying Cummins will come for at least part of 2027, around Australia's Tests and the Ashes. Aakash Chopra wants an overseas opener (Shai Hope is the name Chopra gives), spin back-up and an overseas lower-order batter, and no more fast bowlers.

  • An opener to replace Head. Jake Fraser-McGurk scored at 207 in the powerplay, Nissanka at 169.
  • A spinner. Maheesh Theekshana went at 7.87 an over in the middle overs.
  • An overseas hitter for the lower order, with Livingstone gone. Shimron Hetmyer struck at 154 at the death.

Delhi Capitals

₹39.5 cr for 9 places · won 7 of 14 in 2026

Delhi's powerplay batting was the slowest in the league (8.67 an over) and their middle-overs bowling the most expensive (9.98). KL Rahul opened in all 14 of their innings and is retained. Pathum Nissanka, Rahul's partner in 10 of them (278 runs), has been released, so Delhi keep only 42% of their powerplay runs. The bowling has lost more. Kuldeep Yadav (37 overs) has gone to Lucknow, T Natarajan to Hyderabad and Mitchell Starc has opted out, which leaves Delhi 52% of their middle overs and 55% of their death overs. Against spin in the middle overs they scored at 128, against 159 off pace.

Beyond the numbers

Delhi have a new head coach in Sourav Ganguly, appointed in October, with Yuvraj Singh as assistant and batting coach. The opener beside Rahul may already be in the squad: Prithvi Shaw was retained while Nissanka, Sahil Parakh and Abishek Porel were released, and ESPNcricinfo's Nagraj Gollapudi has reported Yuvraj working closely with Shaw. Shaw played no match in 2026; in 2024, the last IPL season Shaw played, the return was 198 off 121 balls. Ganguly has said Rishabh Pant will bat in the middle order, not open. The captaincy is open: the Times of India reports Axar Patel, who led in 2026, is reluctant to carry on, and Delhi may wait until after the auction. Aakash Chopra expects Delhi to go after Deepak Chahar or Avesh Khan, since the pace looks thin.

  • Pace for both ends. Chahar took 19 powerplay wickets at 8.52 an over from 2024 to 2026; Avesh 19 at the death at 9.91.
  • A middle-overs spinner for Kuldeep's overs. Theekshana went at 7.87.
  • An opener, if Shaw is not the answer. Nissanka scored at 169 in the powerplay and could be bought back.

Mumbai Indians

₹32.6 cr for 9 places, only 2 for overseas players · won 4 of 14 in 2026

Mumbai keep only 46% of their powerplay overs and 38% of the wickets taken in them: Deepak Chahar and Trent Boult were released (Boult took 2 wickets in 15 overs), and Hardik Pandya went to Punjab in the trade that brought Arshdeep Singh, who took 14 wickets for Punjab. Three spinners have been released too: Allah Ghazanfar, who took 13 wickets, Keshav Maharaj and Mayank Markande, leaving Mitchell Santner and Raghu Sharma. At the death they were 8th for scoring at 10.26 an over, and the squad now holds 2 finishers.

Beyond the numbers

Rohit Sharma has been named captain for a 20th season, at 40 next April, four years after last leading the side. The coaching staff has changed as well. Mahela Jayawardene stood down after 2026, and Kieron Pollard is head coach, with Robin Uthappa coaching the batting and Mitchell McClenaghan the bowling. Jasprit Bumrah hurt a knee in July and missed India's Tests in Sri Lanka, but is back in India's Test side. With two overseas places, Aakash Chopra says the buys should be an Indian spinner or an Indian seamer.

  • An Indian spinner for the middle overs. The list is thin here: no Indian spinner on it has bowled 10 or more middle overs since 2024 at under 9 an over.
  • An Indian finisher. Shahrukh Khan struck at 179 at the death, Nehal Wadhera at 160.
  • A third Indian seamer behind Bumrah and Arshdeep. Chahar went at 8.52 in the powerplay and could be bought back.

Chennai Super Kings

₹28.05 cr for 6 places · won 6 of 14 in 2026

Chennai's middle overs were a strength with the ball: second cheapest in the league (8.78 an over) and second for taking wickets, one every 18.9 balls. The new ball was not. Chennai took a powerplay wicket only once every 31.5 balls, 9th, and four of the seamers who bowled it have gone: Khaleel Ahmed (13 powerplay overs) and Nathan Ellis to Hyderabad, Matt Henry and Spencer Johnson released. Chennai keep 64% of their powerplay overs. With the bat they lost powerplay wickets faster than any side, one every 16.8 balls, and were 9th for middle-overs scoring at 8.58 an over. The squad holds only 2 finishers.

Beyond the numbers

Ruturaj Gaikwad stays captain, and MS Dhoni, who did not play in 2026, has retired and become team mentor. Stephen Fleming left after 18 years, and Zaheer Khan is head coach in a first head-coaching job, picked, Chennai's managing director said, for the work Zaheer has done with young fast bowlers. The trade with Hyderabad brought Liam Livingstone and Jack Edwards, an Australian seam-bowling all-rounder. Abhinav Mukund has argued Chennai's biggest need is an Indian spinner; the numbers above put the gap at the new ball instead.

  • A new-ball seamer. Boult (8.26 in the powerplay) or Chahar (8.52).
  • A finisher, if Livingstone does not settle there. Wadhera (160 at the death) or Mahipal Lomror (200).
  • Middle-overs runs against spin, where they scored at 132.

Rajasthan Royals

₹27.05 cr for 6 places · out in the playoffs in 2026

Rajasthan scored fastest of anyone in the powerplay, 11.38 an over, with Yashasvi Jaiswal and Vaibhav Sooryavanshi opening in all 16 innings. After that the wickets came quickly: a wicket every 19.9 balls in the middle overs (9th) and every 10.9 at the death (8th), where they were 7th for scoring at 10.34 an over. Shimron Hetmyer, released, made 72 off 62 balls. Mohammed Shami and Manimaran Siddharth arrive from Lucknow for Sam Curran, who did not play in 2026.

Beyond the numbers

Riyan Parag is confirmed as captain and Kumar Sangakkara stays as head coach, though Parag had shoulder surgery in June and has only lately been batting again. Dhruv Jurel kept wicket all of 2026. Aakash Chopra says the gap is a finisher, with Hetmyer and Curran gone and Ravindra Jadeja not a modern power-hitter; the same piece has Abhinav Mukund seeing Shami and Jofra Archer sharing the new ball.

  • A finisher. Lomror struck at 200 at the death, Wadhera at 160, Hetmyer at 154.
  • A middle-order batter: they hold 2 against the 4 a full squad wants.

Punjab Kings

₹18.6 cr for 6 places · won 7 of 14 in 2026

Punjab scored fastest at the death (12.55 an over) and bowled the most expensive death overs (11.97). Arshdeep Singh, who bowled 26 powerplay and 13 death overs, has gone to Mumbai, so Punjab keep 60% of their powerplay overs and 47% of the wickets in them. Hardik Pandya arrives from Mumbai beside Shreyas Iyer, who led them in 2026. On paper the squad still holds 7 pace bowlers, but a count cannot see what Arshdeep was worth.

Beyond the numbers

Ness Wadia, Punjab's co-owner, said Hardik is a long-term signing, not a stopgap, and that playing under Iyer is no issue. Iyer, now also India's T20I captain, stays in charge with Ricky Ponting as coach. Hardik's bowling is a question, after a back spasm in IPL 2026 and a pause in bowling in September. The fast-bowling coach is new: Paras Mhambrey replaces James Hopes, with the late-innings bowling the job. Cheteshwar Pujara says who replaces Arshdeep decides Punjab's season; Irfan Pathan wants a fast bowler and a back-up for Yuzvendra Chahal.

  • A new-ball seamer. Boult took 21 powerplay wickets at 8.26 from 2024 to 2026.
  • A death bowler. Avesh (19 death wickets) or Pathirana (15).
  • A back-up wrist-spinner for Chahal.

Royal Challengers Bengaluru

₹14.15 cr for 5 places, 1 for an overseas player · champions in 2026

The champions kept every bowler who bowled for them, and ranked in the top four in every phase with the bat. The one soft spot was taking middle-overs wickets: one every 23.8 balls, 7th. At an average of 32.1 theirs is the oldest retained squad in the league, and it holds only 1 middle-order batter and 2 specialist spinners. The main loss is Jacob Bethell, who opened in 7 of their innings.

Beyond the numbers

Bethell was released because of a knee that needs surgery, director of cricket Mo Bobat said, and Bethell has said this IPL will be missed. Bobat wants the auction to add competition for places and injury cover, with little money to do it; head coach Andy Flower is aiming for a third title in a row. Irfan Pathan names a back-up for Josh Hazlewood as the biggest need.

  • Seam cover for Hazlewood. With one overseas place, an Indian: Chahar (8.52 in the powerplay) or Tushar Deshpande (17 death wickets).
  • Top-order cover for Bethell.
  • A wicket-taking spinner for the middle overs.

Gujarat Titans

₹8.75 cr for 3 places, 2 of them for overseas players · runners-up in 2026

Gujarat kept nearly their whole side; the best known of their five releases are Shahrukh Khan and Ishant Sharma. Their bowling took middle-overs wickets more often than anyone, one every 16.2 balls. Their batting at the death was the slowest of all (10.00 an over), and the squad holds just 2 finishers. Harshal Patel arrives from Hyderabad after 13 overs there without a wicket in 2026 at 10.83 an over.

Beyond the numbers

Shubman Gill captains again. Releasing Shahrukh, signed to finish, suggests Gujarat lost patience with the role rather than gave up on it; one preview points to a cheap overseas finisher, which suits the two overseas places. Jos Buttler had hamstring surgery in September; no return date has been given.

  • An overseas death hitter. Of the overseas batters on the list, only Hetmyer and Daryl Mitchell have batted at the death in the IPL since 2024; Hetmyer struck at 154 there, Mitchell at 121.

Every number is counted from the IPL ball-by-ball archive (full-length innings, super overs left out): a wide is not a ball faced, a bowler is charged wides and no-balls but not byes or leg-byes, and only the wickets that are a bowler's own count to them. "Specialist" bowlers are bowlers and all-rounders, not batters who bowl the odd over. Squads, money and places are as the retentions left them on 17 November.
