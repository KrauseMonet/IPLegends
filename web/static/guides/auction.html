The auction

The idea

The auction puts you in charge of one of the ten current IPL franchises with a purse of ₹120 crore. Nine computer-run franchises bid against you for the same players. You need to buy eighteen players, pick your best twelve from them, and then play a full IPL season against the squads the other nine bought. Every player on offer is one real season of one real player, rated on the same 70 to 99 scale as the draft.

Open auction and mega auction

An open auction starts with ten empty squads and full purses. A mega auction follows the rules of the real mega auctions: before bidding starts, each franchise may keep up to five players from its own history, at fixed prices of ₹18 crore, ₹14 crore and ₹11 crore for the first three and ₹18 crore and ₹14 crore for the fourth and fifth. Those prices come straight out of the purse, so keeping a legend means less money for everyone else.

A franchise has six places in all for retentions and Right to Match cards, so every place not used on a retention becomes a card. When one of your franchise's former players is sold to somebody else, you may use a card to match the final price and take him. The buyer then gets one chance to raise the price, and you decide whether to match again.

How bidding works

Players come up in sets: first a marquee set of the biggest names, then sets of batters, all-rounders, wicketkeepers, fast bowlers, spinners and other bowlers, in turn. Each player has a base price from ₹30 lakh to ₹2 crore according to his rating, and bids rise in the official steps: ₹5 lakh at a time below ₹1 crore, then ₹10 lakh, ₹20 lakh and ₹25 lakh as the price grows.

You can bid in two ways. Live bidding raises the price one step at a time against a countdown, as on auction day. Limit bidding lets you name the most you will pay and leave the auctioneer to bid for you up to that figure. Either way the result is the same: the player goes to whoever is willing to pay the most, at one step above the next-highest offer. You can pass on a player, on the rest of a set, or on the whole auction.

Players nobody buys come back in an accelerated round at the end. If a squad still has places to fill, a fill round offers players at the minimum price, and you can sort and filter them by role, batting position and nationality to find the one your twelve still needs.

The rules a squad must meet

  • Eighteen players, of whom twelve will play: eleven and an Impact Player.
  • At most six overseas players in the eighteen, and four in the twelve on the field.
  • A legal twelve must always be possible. You can never place a bid that would leave you unable to field a keeper, five bowling options and a batting order, or without the money to fill your remaining places at the minimum price. The bid box tells you what your squad still needs.

The computer franchises follow the same rules. They value players by rating, pay a little more for their own former players, and never spend more than a quarter of their purse on one man. You are allowed to, if you think he is worth it.

After the hammer

When every squad is full, the morning paper gives its verdict on the auction: the record buy, the bidding wars, the steals and the splurges, and every squad ranked by the strength of its best twelve. A board brief at the start sets you three targets for the auction and the season. They are for pride only; they change nothing in the game.

You then choose your twelve and play the season, exactly as in the draft: fourteen league matches, the IPL playoffs, and super overs for ties. At the end the season's verdict judges your buys: the bargain of the auction, your best value, and the expensive signing who did least.

Advice for the auction

  • Keep money for the end. A big early buy feels good, but the last places in a squad often cost more than expected if you are short of a keeper or a bowler.
  • Watch your overseas count. Many of the strongest seasons belong to overseas players, and six places run out quickly.
  • Value fits, not names. A 90-rated opener is less use to a squad that already has two good openers than an 85-rated death bowler.
  • Use Right to Match cards late. They let you wait and see what a former player sells for, then decide.

Auctions with friends

An auction room lets several people bid live against each other, with the computer franchises filling the other seats. The host chooses an open or mega auction and whether to allow a trade window after the auction, in which two people can swap players one for one. Each lot has a bidding clock that is topped up whenever someone bids, so the last bidder always leaves the others time to answer.
