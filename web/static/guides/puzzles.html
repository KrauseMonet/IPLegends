The puzzles

Five daily puzzles and a quiz

Each day brings five new puzzles built from the IPL's real record, the same for everybody, plus Flashback, a quiz you can play any time. None of them needs an account. If you sign in, your daily results go on a leaderboard and count towards badges on your profile. Finishing any daily puzzle keeps your streak going. Every puzzle can also be played as practice on other days' grids.

Bingo

A three-by-three grid has a franchise or a feat along each row and column: played for Chennai Super Kings, say, and took four wickets in an innings. Fill a square by naming a player who fits both. You have nine guesses for nine squares and each player can be used only once. A square that very few players fit is worth more points than an easy one, so a grid's score rewards knowing the deep cuts.

A tip: fill the hard squares first, while you still have every player available, and save the famous names for the squares where they are the only answer you know.

Play Bingo

Guess the Player

There is a mystery IPL player and you have eight guesses to find him. Each guess shows how the player you named compares with the mystery man: the clubs they share, his role, his country, his bowling, the season he started and finished, and his career runs and wickets. Green means a match, yellow means close, and an arrow says whether the mystery player's number is higher or lower.

A tip: an early guess with a long career at several clubs tells you the most, because it gives you something to compare on every tile.

Play Guess the Player

Common Ground

Two players are shown. Name everyone who was ever a teammate of both, meaning they were in the same squad in the same season. You have four minutes. Each player you find is worth 100 points, a wrong guess costs 20, and finding the whole set adds a bonus for every second left on the clock. A miss tells you whether the man you named played with one of the pair, which helps you narrow the search.

Play Common Ground

Name the XI

A famous match's scorecard is shown with the names blanked out: a final, a match with a hundred, or one decided in a super over. Each blank keeps its batting position and what the player did that day, such as 69 off 38 balls or three wickets for 45. Name the side. Five mistakes end the game, and if you name a man who played for the other team that day, the game tells you so.

Play Name the XI

Three to Make

You are given a target and a statistic, such as 9,000 career runs. In ninety seconds, name three players whose figures add up as close to the target as you can manage. The figures stay hidden until you lock in, so it is a test of what you remember rather than of looking things up. The closest answer wins; level answers are separated first by picking less obvious players, and then by speed.

Play Three to Make

Flashback

Ten quick questions on IPL history: which season had these leading scorers, who top-scored for a team, which franchise a player was at that year, which of two big seasons was bigger. Every wrong option is a real figure too, so you cannot rule anything out by guessing what looks made up. Faster answers and streaks of right answers score more, and a quiz can be shared with a friend so they face the same ten questions.

Play Flashback

Leaderboards and rarity

Each daily puzzle has its own leaderboard, and an overall board adds up how close you came to the best score in each game that day. In Bingo, Common Ground and Name the XI, picking a player few others picked earns a rarity bonus, but only once at least twenty people have finished that day's puzzle, so a small crowd can never make a pick look rarer than it is.
